Reward iThe episode return reported by the trainer. Follow its trend across many episodes rather than judging one sample: exploration, random resets, and multiple agents can make individual values noisy. The absolute scale and sign are task-specific, so a negative return does not automatically mean that learning has failed.

Progress (mean / max) iProgress is defined by the Godot scenario and usually normalized from 0 to 1. Mean summarizes the controlled agents, while max shows the best result in that episode. A rising mean indicates broader improvement; a high max with a low mean means success is still occasional. Confirm the exact meaning with the scene's ProgressProvider.

Losses (critic / actor) iCritic loss measures value-target fitting; actor loss is the policy objective optimized by actor-critic methods. Their scale and even the actor loss sign depend on the algorithm and reward scale. Look for sustained instability, rapid growth, or non-finite values rather than a universal target number. Short spikes can be normal after new experience enters training.

Throughput (env steps/s · updates/s) iEnvironment steps/s measures simulation decisions collected each second; updates/s measures learner optimization steps. These values describe speed, not policy quality. Compare them after warmup under the same configuration. A persistent drop can point to slower Godot instances, rendering overhead, queue pressure, checkpoint work, or a learner bottleneck.

Alpha (entropy coeff) iSAC's learned entropy temperature. A higher alpha gives more weight to stochastic exploration; a lower alpha favors the reward objective and a more concentrated policy. There is no universal ideal value: interpret it together with entropy, rewards, and action behavior. Abrupt runaway growth or an early collapse toward zero may indicate unstable temperature tuning.

Outcomes (success · collision · stall) iRates reported for the controlled agents in each completed episode. Success means the scene emitted its finish condition; collision and stall reflect scene-defined terminal diagnostics. Healthy training usually raises success while reducing avoidable failures, but the categories are not guaranteed to be mutually exclusive. Check the scenario event rules for their exact meaning.
